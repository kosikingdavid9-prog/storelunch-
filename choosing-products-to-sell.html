Choosing Products to Sell

Choosing the right product is one of the first important decisions when starting an online store. A good product should solve a real problem or satisfy a genuine customer need.

1. Start with a customer

Instead of choosing a product simply because it looks popular, start by thinking about the person you want to sell to. A clear understanding of your potential customer can help you identify products that solve a real problem or meet a specific need.

  • Decide who the product is intended for.
  • Think about what that customer needs or struggles with.
  • Consider why they would choose your product instead of another option.

2. Look for real demand

A product is more promising when there is evidence that people actually want it. Look beyond temporary trends and try to understand whether customers are consistently searching for, discussing, or buying similar products.

  • Look for existing products and businesses serving the same need.
  • Pay attention to customer questions and complaints.
  • Check whether interest appears consistent rather than based only on a short-term trend.

3. Think about competition

Competition can show that a market already exists, but it also means you need to understand what other sellers are offering. Study their products, pricing, customer experience, and how they present their businesses. Look for ways to make your own offer clear and useful without simply copying another seller.

4. Consider your costs

A product can have strong demand and still be difficult to sell profitably if the costs are too high. Before committing to a product, estimate the main costs involved in getting it to the customer.

  • Product or supplier cost
  • Packaging and delivery costs
  • Marketing and advertising expenses
  • Platform or payment-related fees

5. Think about how you will market it

A product is easier to build a business around when you have a realistic way to reach potential customers. Think about where your target customers spend time and what type of content would make them interested in the product.

  • Identify the platforms where your potential customers are active.
  • Consider how you will explain the product's value.
  • Choose marketing methods that fit your audience and budget.

6. Test before going big

You do not need to commit a large amount of money before knowing whether a product has potential. Start with a small test, learn from the response, and use what you discover to decide what to improve before expanding.

  • Start with a manageable quantity or small test.
  • Pay attention to customer questions and feedback.
  • Review your costs and results before investing more.
  • Improve your approach based on what you learn.
